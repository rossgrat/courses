1. Mission and target candidate
The primary goal is to become an exceptionally strong candidate for low-latency C++ / execution
engineering roles at quantitative trading firms.
Secondary paths remain deliberately open:

Trading strategy engineering

Trading infrastructure

Platform engineering

Performance engineering

Market-data engineering

Hardware/software acceleration
The curriculum is asynchronous and mastery-gated. There are no arbitrary week counts. You advance
when you can demonstrate the skill.
The course also contains explicit application gates so studying cannot expand indefinitely and
prevent you from actually interviewing.
The target candidate
At the end, your profile should read approximately like this:
Senior backend/distributed-systems engineer with strong C++ systems-programming skills,
practical knowledge of Linux internals, networking, concurrency and computer architecture, and
demonstrated ability to measure and optimize latency-sensitive systems. Built a realistic electronic-
trading system in C++ and contributed to production-quality open-source systems software.
That is much stronger than:
Backend engineer who studied some C++ and did LeetCode.
You do not need to pretend you already have professional HFT experience. A credible story is:
I have spent my career building backend and distributed systems. I became increasingly interested
in performance-sensitive systems, so I deliberately developed much deeper
C++/Linux/performance expertise and built real systems demonstrating it.

Exchange simulator

17. Anti-rabbit-hole rules
1. Do not learn advanced material merely because it exists.
Learn it when your project or target job
demands it.
2. Never read three books before building something.
Study enough to build. Build. Then return to
theory.
3. Measurement before optimization.
Never optimize Mercury because HFT systems probably do this.
Profile it.
4. Correct before lock-free.
A mutex-based implementation is often the correct first implementation.
5. Do not postpone applications for FPGA.
FPGA is optional specialization.
6. Do not postpone applications for quantitative finance.
You are applying as an engineer.
7. Do not chase obscure C++ trivia.
Deep fundamentals beat trivia.
8. Do not solve LeetCode forever.
Interview problems are a means to an end.
9. Every deep dive must justify itself.
Will this improve interview performance, the project, an open-
source contribution, or ability to do the job? If not, defer it.

21. What exceptionally strong means
You will not differentiate yourself merely because you know C++ syntax.
The differentiator is: I can explain what the hardware is doing, write the C++ that interacts with it,
measure the resulting system, identify the bottleneck, improve it, and defend the tradeoffs.
Combined with existing backend/distributed-systems experience, this creates a valuable hybrid:
distributed-systems maturity + low-level systems competence + performance engineering
+ trading-domain experience from substantial personal work
That is a much stronger positioning than attempting to compete with new graduates solely on algorithm
puzzles.
22. Definition of done
There is intentionally no final point where you "know low latency." The course succeeds when:
1.
You have crossed the application gate.
2.
You are actively interviewing.
3.
Interview failures become input for further study.
4.
You continue improving Mercury.
5.
You make meaningful open-source contributions.
6.
Your study becomes driven increasingly by real interview feedback.
